1 Abstract

This vignette sets out what the three filters compute: the HP filter as a penalised least-squares problem, the criterion of the modified HP filter and the exact cross-validation it approximates, the weights, gains and end-point loss that measure the end-point bias, and the end-point schemes of the fully modified HP filter. It also says how the package computes them, in \(O(T)\) operations per smoothing parameter.

2 The HP filter

With the series \(\vx = (x_1, \dots, x_T)^\top\) and the \((T - 2) \times T\) matrix \(\mK\) of second differences (rows \((1, -2, 1)\)), the trend minimises \(\lVert\vx - \vg\rVert^2 + \lambda\lVert\mK\vg\rVert^2\), so \[ \hat{\vg} = (\mI + \lambda\mA)^{-1}\vx = \mB\vx, \qquad \mA = \mK^\top\mK , \] and \(\hat{\vc} = \vx - \hat{\vg}\). Because \(\mK\vone = \mathbf{0}\), every row of \(\mB\) sums to one: the trend is a weighted average of the observations.

Computation. \(\mI + \lambda\mA\) is symmetric, positive definite and pentadiagonal. The package factors it as \(\mL\mD\mL^\top\) with a unit lower-triangular \(\mL\) of bandwidth two and solves in \(O(T)\) operations; version 0.1.0 solved a dense \(T \times T\) system, \(O(T^3)\), for every \(\lambda\) of the search.

3 Choosing lambda from the data

3.1 The criterion of the modified HP filter

McDermott (1997) chooses \(\lambda\) by cross-validation: leave out one observation, predict it from the trend of the others, and sum the squared errors. Generalised cross-validation (Craven and Wahba 1979) replaces the leave-one-out errors by \[ \mathrm{GCV}(\lambda) = \frac{T^{-1}\sum_t\left(x_t - \hat{g}_t(\lambda)\right)^2}{\left(1 - T^{-1}\tr\mB(\lambda)\right)^2} \] (Choudhary et al. 2014, eq. 5), and the papers and their code use the approximation \[ \mathrm{GCV}(\lambda) \approx \frac{1}{T}\left(1 + \frac{2T}{\lambda}\right)\sum_t\left(x_t - \hat{g}_t(\lambda)\right)^2 \] (their eq. 6, attributed to Silverman 1984). mhp_filter() minimises the approximation over \(\lambda = 1, \dots,\) max_lambda by default, and the exact criterion with criterion = "exact": the eigenvalues \(\mu_i\) of \(\mA\), computed once, give \(\tr\mB(\lambda) = \sum_i (1 + \lambda\mu_i)^{-1}\) and the residual sum of squares for every \(\lambda\) in \(O(T)\) operations.

  )
c(approximate = ap$lambda, exact = ex$lambda)

The exact criterion falls steadily as \(\lambda\) falls, down to \(\lambda = 1\), where the trend almost interpolates the series: generalised cross-validation is known to undersmooth series whose deviations from the trend are autocorrelated, as business cycles are. The approximation adds a much heavier penalty for small \(\lambda\) (at \(\lambda = 683\) the factor \(1 + 2T/\lambda\) is 1.59, against 1.17 for the exact one), and that penalty, not cross-validation as such, gives the modified HP filter its interior minimum. The package keeps the published approximation as the default.

4 The end-point bias

4.1 Weights

Row \(t\) of \(\mB\) holds the weights of \(\hat{g}_t\). In the middle of the series they are symmetric; near the ends they are not, and the end observation gets a large weight in its own trend (Hanif et al. 2017, Figure 1).

w <-
  hp_weights(
      x      = 50
    , lambda = 100
    , form   = "rows"
    , rows   = NULL
    )
plot(x = w)

4.2 Gains

The gain of \(\hat{g}_t\) at frequency \(\omega\), \[ P_t(\omega) = \left|\sum_{j=1}^{T} b_{tj}\,e^{i\omega(j - t)}\right| , \] is the factor by which the trend scales an oscillation of that frequency (eq. 6 of Hanif et al. 2017). In the middle the gain falls to zero at high frequencies; at the ends it does not, so the trend keeps part of the cycle.

g <-
  hp_gain(
      x      = 50
    , lambda = 100
    , form   = "rows"
    , theta  = seq(from = 0, to = pi, length.out = 101)
    , rows   = NULL
    )
plot(x = g)

The package computes \(\sum_j b_{tj}e^{i\omega j}\) for all \(t\) at once as \(\mB\cos(\omega\cdot)\) and \(\mB\sin(\omega\cdot)\): two solves per frequency, without forming \(\mB\).

4.3 Loss

Bloechl (2014) measures the distortion by the distance of each gain from the gain at the centre \(c\) of the series, \[ l(t) = \theta\sum_{\omega}\left[P_c(\omega) - P_t(\omega)\right]^2, \qquad L = \sum_{t=1}^{T} l(t), \] with \(\omega = 0, 0.1, \dots, 3.1\) and \(\theta = 0.1\) (eqs. 7 and 8 of Hanif et al. 2017). It depends on \(T\) and the smoothing parameters only.

hl <-
  hp_loss(
      x      = 50
    , lambda = 100
    , form   = "rows"
    , theta  = seq(from = 0, to = 3.1, by = 0.1)
    , weight = 0.1
    )
plot(x = hl)

5 The fully modified HP filter

5.1 End-point schemes

Hanif et al. (2017) raise the smoothing parameter of the \(k\) values at each end: \(\lambda_t = \lambda\) in the middle and \(\lambda + \alpha j^{i}\) for the \(j\)-th value from the middle, linear (\(i = 1\)) or quadratic (\(i = 2\)).

5.2 Two forms of the weights

The published MATLAB code multiplies row \(t\) of \(\mA\) by \(\lambda_t\): \[ \hat{\vg} = (\mI + \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_t)\mA)^{-1}\vx \qquad \text{(\texttt{form = "rows"}, the default)}. \] This matrix is not symmetric, but \(\mI + \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_t)\mA = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_t) (\mathrm{diag}(1/\lambda_t) + \mA)\), so the package solves the symmetric pentadiagonal system \((\mathrm{diag}(1/\lambda_t) + \mA)\hat{\vg} = \mathrm{diag}(1/\lambda_t)\vx\). The penalised least-squares problem with one smoothing parameter per second difference, \(\lVert\vx - \vg\rVert^2 + \sum_r\lambda_r(\mK\vg)_r^2\), gives instead \[ \hat{\vg} = (\mI + \mK^\top\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_r)\mK)^{-1}\vx \qquad \text{(\texttt{form = "differences"})}, \] the form in which Bloechl (2014) and eq. 9 of the paper write the scheme. Both are \(O(T)\); for a constant \(\lambda\) they coincide.

y <- us_macro$gdp
rows <-
  fmhp_filter(
      x               = y
    , max_lambda      = 3000
    , k_linear        = 15
    , k_quadratic     = NULL
    , alpha_linear    = seq(from = 0, to = 2000, by = 10)
    , alpha_quadratic = seq(from = 0, to = 2000, by = 5)
    , schemes         = c("linear", "quadratic")

5.3 The procedure

  1. \(\lambda^{\mathrm{MHP}}\) of the modified HP filter.
  2. For the linear and the quadratic scheme, the weight \(\alpha\) of a grid that minimises \(L\) at \(\lambda^{\mathrm{MHP}}\).
  3. With that \(\alpha\), \(\lambda^{\mathrm{FMHP}}\) minimising the criterion of the modified HP filter again.
  4. The scheme with the smaller loss in step 2 gives the trend and the cycle.

The defaults are those of the MATLAB code: \(\lambda \le 3000\); \(k = 15\) and \(\alpha \in \{0, 10, \dots, 2000\}\) for the linear scheme; \(k = \mathrm{round}(\min(22, T/2 - 5))\) and \(\alpha \in \{0, 5, \dots, 2000\}\) for the quadratic one; logarithms. The text of the paper states \(k = 20\); k_linear and k_quadratic set it. MATLAB rounds halves up, and so does the package (the centre \(c\) of the loss and the quadratic \(k\)); vignette 4 shows that this reproduces the published losses.
