1 Abstract

Hanif, Iqbal and Choudhary (2017) compare the HP, modified HP and fully modified HP filters on 30 simulated designs and report that the fully modified filter estimates the cycle better. This vignette repeats the comparison with fmhp_simulation() for quarterly series and for series averaged to annual data, with linear and nonlinear trends and both forms of the end-point weights. The results are precomputed by tools/simulation-study.R and shipped in inst/extdata; the vignette reads them, so it builds in seconds.

rmse  <- data.table::fread(system.file("extdata", "simulation_rmse.csv", package = "mhpfilter"))
power <- data.table::fread(system.file("extdata", "simulation_power.csv", package = "mhpfilter"))
n_rep <- 20

4 Reading the results

The tables above are the evidence; the points below only summarise them.

  • The paper reports (Table 1) that the fully modified filter beats the HP filter in 100% of the replicates of every design and every setting. Here it has the smaller RMSE in 20% of the replicates for quarterly series with a linear trend and in 6% for series averaged to annual data, and it beats the HP filter in more than half of the replicates in 7 of the 240 combinations of design and setting.
  • The difference is largest at the ends, where the method is meant to help: for quarterly series with a linear trend the RMSE at the ends is 4.72 for the HP filter and 5.66 for the fully modified filter, against 5.19 and 5.16 in the middle.
  • The loss that chooses the end-point weights measures how far the gain at each date is from the gain in the middle; it does not depend on the data and does not measure how close the estimated cycle is to the true one. A likely reason for the result: raising \(\lambda\) near the ends makes the trend stiffer there, so the estimated cycle takes in more of the trend shocks.
  • The filter used here is the one of the authors’ MATLAB code (the tests check it to \(10^{-8}\)), so the difference does not come from the implementation. It can come from the design: the paper does not fully report how its trends were drawn, the length of its series or how its RMSE was computed, so these results are not a reproduction of its Tables 1 and 2. They are 20 replicates per design; the shares are rough to a few percentage points.
  • The two forms of the end-point weights give similar results.

To rerun the study with more replicates:

# from the package folder; 100 replicates take about three hours
system2(command = "Rscript", args = c("tools/simulation-study.R", "100"))
